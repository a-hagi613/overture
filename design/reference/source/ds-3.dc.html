9Voice

Plain and direct. Short, neutral sentences. Say what Overture does and what you control.

Do

  • Plain section titles: How it works, Where it applies, Pricing, Questions.
  • One short sentence, then stop.
  • Concrete verbs: finds, scores, answers, writes, submits, tracks.
  • Name the control: "Anything it is unsure about waits for you."
  • Fictional sample data is labelled "Sample data".

Don't

  • No hype, no superlatives, no exclamation points.
  • No emoji. No ornate or legal-sounding titles. No § or glyph numbering.
  • No invented customers, metrics, testimonials or user counts.
  • No internal words in the UI: Brain, Hands, runner, lease, provider, proxy.
  • No dating-app language for sorting: Overture decides, you do not swipe.

Names

HoldA question Overture could not answer from your facts. You answer it once and every waiting application moves forward.

Claims

Claims follow the product roadmap the owner approves.

Say only what the shipped build does. Today the landing shows Overture applying through Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS and Taleo, because the owner approved that as the build target. When the roadmap changes, the copy changes with it; never promise a system that is not on the approved list, and never promise a result, only the work Overture does.

10Accessibility

Built into the design, not added after.

RuleSpec
Text contrast4.5:1 minimum for all text, including 11 px grey labels; 3:1 from 24 px. See the contrast table. Small orange text always uses signal-text.
Not by colour aloneStatuses also differ in lightness, wording or shape: Hold is a labelled pill, source tags differ by outline style, the active tab is underlined, the active nav item has a marker.
Target size44 px minimum on touch: phone buttons 48, tab bar 60, stage pills 44, icon buttons 44, toggles 44. Pointer-only desktop controls are 40 px.
Focus2 px solid ink ring with a 2 to 3 px offset on every interactive element (--ring); never removed, never orange.
Real elementsButton, a, input with a label (or aria-label). Icon-only buttons have an aria-label. Decorative SVG is aria-hidden; informative SVG has role img and a label.
Theme controlA real button with a state-aware aria-label: "Switch to dark mode" and "Switch to light mode".
MotionReduced-motion users get the static state of every animation; no motion is needed to understand anything. Nothing flashes.
Language and orderPlain words, one H1 per page, headings in order, reading order matches visual order (the path is numbered).
